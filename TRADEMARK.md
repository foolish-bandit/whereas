# Whereas Trademark Policy

The Whereas software is licensed under AGPL-3.0-or-later, which means you can copy, modify, and redistribute it freely under the terms of that license. The trademark policy below covers the **name** "Whereas" and associated logos, which are not covered by the software license.

## What you can do without permission

- Use the name "Whereas" to refer to the unmodified upstream project.
- Say your product "uses Whereas" or "is built on Whereas," provided this is accurate and not misleading.
- Distribute unmodified copies of Whereas under the name "Whereas."
- Use the name in academic papers, blog posts, talks, and documentation.

## What requires permission

- Distributing a modified version of Whereas under the name "Whereas" or any confusingly similar name.
- Using "Whereas" in the name of your product, company, or service.
- Using the Whereas logo in a way that suggests endorsement.

If you fork Whereas and modify it, please pick a different name. Suggested patterns: "MyFirm Contracts (powered by Whereas)" or "Acme CLM, a Whereas distribution."

## Why this exists

Open-source trademark policies aren't a contradiction. The AGPL protects software freedom — your right to use, study, modify, and share the code. Trademark protects users from confusion about which version of the software they're getting and who's responsible for it. Both can coexist.

If you're unsure whether your use is permitted, open an issue or email the maintainers. We'd rather say yes than send a cease-and-desist.
